# Notes: publishing on Google Play (for information only)

Decided: these notes are a reference. Nothing here is built into the choir app. A separate task will explore option 3.

## Three ways
1. **Own choir only, as a Play app.** A thin Android wrapper (Trusted Web Activity) around the existing website. Small effort, no extra server cost. Google charges a one-time developer fee (about $25). New personal accounts may need a closed test with real testers before they can publish. The same app already installs from the browser for free.
2. **A multi-choir product for other teachers.** Needs a rebuild: sign-in and separate data per choir, teacher billing, a bigger server (this breaks the free hosting budget), support and responsibility for other people's children's data.
3. **The Vocals game as its own app.** The most self-contained part: a free level plus a paid year, for any child or singer. Much simpler than option 2. This is the one to explore in a separate task.

## Pros of Google Play
- Easy to find and install; feels trustworthy.
- Google handles billing, refunds and payouts.
- Updates reach users automatically.

## Cons
- Google keeps a cut (about 15% on subscriptions for small developers; check the current rate).
- Digital features sold inside an app generally have to use Google Play Billing. The current "pay by UPI, teacher unlocks by hand" flow may not be allowed inside a Play app. Rules differ by country and change: check current policy.
- Apps for or used by children have extra rules: privacy policy, Data safety form, no ad trackers, careful handling of children's data. India's data protection law adds parental consent for children's data.
- Review, policy updates, support and possible removal if a rule is broken.
- More users cost more to host (the "$2 a month" rule would need a subscription to cover it).

## How to get paid
- Through Google Play: set up a Google payments profile with a bank account; Google pays monthly after a small threshold, minus its cut. Tax details needed (ask an accountant about GST and income tax on digital services).
- Outside the app: UPI or invoice from teachers or schools. Works for a few known choirs; manual.
- Sell to organisations (a church or school) at one price for all its choirs.

## Suggested path
1. Keep the own choir on the web app as it is.
2. Try option 3 as a small standalone Vocals app, offered to a few teachers by invitation, to see whether they would pay.
3. Only if that works, consider a full multi-choir version.
